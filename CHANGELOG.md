# Changelog

Each version's section becomes the description of its GitHub release.

## v1.2.0
- **Map layers from Rust's own map file.** Rust keeps the full world of every server you've joined in its `maps` folder. RustWatch finds the one for the server it's showing (same size, monuments in the same places) and adds map layers: every large rock formation (god rocks, anvils and the rest), caves with their size and difficulty, train tunnel entrances, monument no-build zones, beaches (where fresh spawns land), biomes, and an estimated cargo ship route. Rust doesn't label which rock model is a god rock or an anvil, so click one on the map and name its model once; every map then shows it. It only reads the file, never the game. If you haven't joined the server in Rust yet, the layers appear once you do.
- **Base cameras.** Watch CCTV, PTZ cameras, auto turrets and drones through Rust+ on the new **Cameras** page: give the camera an ID at a computer station in game and add it here. You can look around with PTZ cameras, aim, fire and reload turrets, and fly drones (WASD, Space up, Ctrl down). It shows when a player is in view. Rust+ only streams cameras while you're not on the server.
- **Automations and raid mode.** Switch smart switches on or off by themselves when a smart alarm goes off or a raid is heard, optionally turning them back after some minutes. **Raid mode** runs a set of switches at once from a button on the Devices page or `!raidmode` / `!raidmode off` in team chat.
- **Loot log.** Every change in a box with a storage monitor is logged (what went in, what came out), with an alert when a box is suddenly emptied. `!loot` shows the latest changes. Upkeep now also shows the clock time the base decays (`!upkeep` too).
- **Enemy patterns.** On the Players page: a **When they play** heatmap of the hours each tracked player (or everyone with a tag) is online over the last two weeks, **Likely teams** from players who keep joining or leaving within seconds of each other (track a whole group in one click), and an alert when **every player with a tag goes offline**.
- **Server stats.** A new page with population over the last week, average players by hour with the busiest and quietest times, and countdowns to the next forced wipe and since this server's wipe. `!wipe` includes the forced wipe.
- **Team trails and deaths on the map.** Dashed lines show where each teammate has been in the last hour, and a layer marks where the team died this wipe.
- **Two-way Discord.** With a Discord bot token and channel (Settings → Discord bot), your team can run RustWatch commands from Discord, team chat can be copied into the channel, and Discord messages can be posted into team chat.
- **Raid costs.** A Reference page with the explosives and sulfur for every wall, door and deployable (vanilla figures from September 2026), a raid planner that adds up a whole raid and finds the cheapest mix, and `!raidcost <wall or door> [how many]` in team chat.
- **New look, styled after Rust's own menus.** Rust's typeface, dark panels, square item slots and green action buttons. The side menu is grouped (In game, Intel, Base, Reference) and each page shows a live count beside its name: team online, tracked players online, unread team chat, new alerts, an alarm going off or upkeep running low, and RAID while a raid is being heard. Number keys 1–0 switch pages, like the hotbar in game. The top bar shows population, in-game time, time since wipe and team online as bars like Rust's health and water bars. Every page opens with a summary row (for example online / AFK / dead for the team, or switches on and lowest upkeep for devices). Storage monitors show a full slot grid with counts like the game's inventory. Items shows crafting cost and recycler output as item slots. Raid explosions are listed with a small compass for the direction. Settings has a section index, and the Save button stays in view. A raid card appears at the top of the overview while one is being heard.
- **Raid callouts.** RustWatch listens for C4, rockets, HV rockets, satchels, beancans, catapult, cannon and MLRS explosions. When 4 or more within a minute agree on a spot, it posts an estimated grid to team chat, for example `Raid heard: 6 explosions (mostly 4 C4, 2 rockets) in 40s, ~250m NE of you, near K12`. It updates while the raid goes on, says when the sounds stop, and marks the area on the map. Direction comes from your left/right audio plus the way you're facing in Rust+, and distance from whether the game played the close, distant or really-distant version of the sound, so it's a rough area. Normal stereo can't tell front from back, so at first it may give two spots and narrow it down as you turn. Gunfire, grenades, fireworks and monument music are ignored. `!raid` gives the latest estimate. Turn it off in Sounds → Raid detection.
- **Hears events in game.** Rust no longer sends cargo, heli, oil rigs or the vendor to Rust+, so RustWatch now listens to Rust's own audio and recognises event sounds: cargo ship, Small and Large Oil Rig being called, the giant excavator, Deep Sea, and nearby Bradley explosions, heli crashes and the travelling vendor. Teach each sound once on the new **Sounds** page: capture the last 30 seconds of game audio (or **import a WAV file** of the sound), drag across the picture to mark the sound, check it and save. After that each sound it hears feeds the event timers (oil rig crate, Bradley respawn, cargo and vendor windows), sends an alert and answers `!cargo`, `!oil`, `!bradley`, `!vendor`, `!excavator` and `!deepsea`. Rust's own server-wide event sounds (cargo coming in, an oil rig resetting, the excavator starting, the Deep Sea alarm) are recognised even when they're quiet under gunfire. Bradley and heli explosions are much less reliable, because they sound like other explosions. On Windows 11 it records only Rust, so music, Discord and videos can't trigger it. Older Windows records all PC audio. Audio is kept in memory only, apart from the clips you save.
- **Seen in game list with real names.** While Rust is running and you're on the server, the Players page lists the people Steam says you recently played with: real Steam names and profiles from your own Steam client, the same list as Shift+Tab → Players. That works even on servers that hide names publicly. Press **Track** on anyone there to follow them by Steam profile. People you already track by name are linked to their Steam profile automatically when they show up. RustWatch only connects to Steam while Rust is running and never touches the game itself.
- **Track players by Steam profile.** Paste someone's Steam profile link into *Track player*, or press **Link Steam** on someone you already track. RustWatch checks Steam every minute for which server they're on, so it works on servers that hide names (Rust's `server.censorplayerlist`, on by default), on servers that won't share their player list, and after a name change. Needs the free Steam Web API key (Settings → Steam) and a public Steam profile.
- Each Steam-linked player shows what Steam says they're doing: on this server, in Rust on another server, offline, or a private profile.
- **Shops and live events:** Rust's 6 August 2026 update stopped servers sending vending machines, cargo, patrol heli and the travelling vendor to Rust+, so no Rust+ app can show them any more. RustWatch now says so on the Shops page, map and Overview, and in `!buy`, `!sell`, `!cargo`, `!heli` and `!vendor`, instead of looking broken. Spawn windows are still estimated from the wipe and server restarts.
- Fixed the hidden-names notice: BattleMetrics *can* show real names when the server owner has given it admin access, but that feed isn't public.

## v1.1.3
- **Player tracker:** recognises servers that hide real player names (Rust's `server.censorplayerlist`) and says so, instead of showing random stand-in names as if they were real.
- **Player tracker:** shows when a server only sends part of its player list (e.g. "85 of 211").
- Includes everything from 1.1.1 and 1.1.2 below.

## v1.1.2
- **Player tracker:** retries when part of a big player list gets lost, handles both packet formats servers use, and explains clearly when a server won't share its player list (player and queue counts still show).
- **Shops:** shows what Rust+ is sending (markers, shops, last error) and explains when the server reports no shops.
- **Diagnostics:** Settings → Files → Save diagnostics writes a troubleshooting file (no passwords or keys).

## v1.1.1
- Shop diagnostics (included in 1.1.2).

## v1.1.0
- First public release: team tracking, live map, world events with spawn forecasts, shop search, smart devices, player tracker, team-chat commands, Steam kill stats, Discord alerts and in-app updates.
